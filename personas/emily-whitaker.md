# Emily Whitaker — Director of Donor Relations and Stewardship, US liberal arts college

**Archetype:** Operational Champion (small-team variant)
**Motion:** B
**Tier:** Core
**Engine:** Connect + Nurture

## Identity

38-46 years old. 4 years current role, 14 years sector. Private liberal arts college in New England. 22,000 alumni, 9,000 active donors, strong reunion culture, high participation expectations. Advancement team of 14. Career: alumni relations → stewardship communications → donor relations. MFA writing before entering advancement.

## A day in the life

Tuesday 8:41am. Opens stewardship tracker first. Reunion is six weeks away. Endowment reports behind. President wants donor stories for a board dinner. Annual Fund wants language for class agents. Major Gifts wants custom impact note for scholarship donor visiting Friday.

Opens Slate Advancement, then Google Drive, then last year's endowment report folder. Data is technically there but not usable without three follow-up emails. Scholarship office has student updates. Finance has fund balances. Major Gifts has donor context. Alumni Relations has reunion attendance. None of it meets in one place.

Proud of the handwritten note project her team finished last week. One alumnus wrote back saying it was the first time the college had made his annual gift feel connected to a student. Has not gotten to stewardship ROI. VP keeps asking whether personalized reporting improves retention. Emily believes it does. Cannot prove it cleanly.

On her mind: "We are built on relationships, but I spend half my week assembling proof that relationships happened."

## Top 3 frustrations

1. Impact data arrives late, partial, and in the wrong format
2. Stewardship gets praised emotionally, then budgeted as overhead
3. High participation culture creates volume without enough signal

## Where she spends attention

CASE Donor Relations, ADRP, liberal arts peer networks. LinkedIn lurker, saves good stewardship examples. Advice circle: Annual Fund director, VP Advancement, registrar/scholarship office contact, peer at another LAC.

## What makes her say yes

- **Demo trigger:** AI4Love shows stewardship opportunities tied to actual participation patterns, not only gift amount
- **Champion moment:** Proves a donor's full relationship: reunion attendance, volunteer work, annual gifts, scholarship fund, recent email engagement
- **Killer objection:** Any tool that drafts donor-facing content without human review
- **Unspoken concern:** AI will flatten the college's voice

## Buying authority

Under $10K USD alone for stewardship tools. $10K-$25K requires VP. Any CRM integration requires Adv Services/IT. Board/procurement rare at her level unless enterprise-wide. Blessing: VP Advancement, Adv Services, sometimes Communications.

## Triggering events

- Organizational: reunion cycle begins, endowment reporting backlog grows, Annual Fund participation falls
- Personal: asked to prove stewardship ROI, major donor complains about generic reporting, beloved donor dies before proper recognition happens

## Quote

> "The college remembers people beautifully in person, then loses the thread in the systems."

2. **No campaign launches:** strong. Stewardship is precise work; automation would feel wrong.

## Strategic note

Emily's MFA-writing background is significant. She processes language carefully. Demo and sales materials should not use generic AI marketing language. Lead with restraint, specificity, and respect for institutional voice. Reference "drafts not sends" if Refusal 3 needs softening for her.
