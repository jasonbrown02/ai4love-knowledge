# Heather MacDonald — Executive Director, small Canadian hospital foundation

**Archetype:** Small-Shop Sleeper Champion
**Motion:** B
**Tier:** Core
**Engine:** Connect + Insights

## Identity

50-58 years old. 5 years current role, 21 years sector. Small Canadian hospital foundation in BC. $3.5M annual revenue, 7,500 donor records, 2,800 active supporters. Four full-time staff. Career: community events → annual giving manager → ED after leading a successful equipment campaign. College diploma in communications, AFP and CAGP training.

## A day in the life

Tuesday 8:27am. Opens email first, then bank deposit report. Gift processor is off sick, so she checks whether weekend donations were entered before finance committee package goes out.

Opens DonorPerfect, then Mailchimp, then a CanadaHelps CSV. A retired nurse who volunteered at the gala made a $500 online gift, but Heather only notices because she recognizes the name. System did not flag it. No one had time.

Proud foundation feels trusted in town. Board members know donors by name. Hospital CEO answers her calls. Gala sold out. Has not gotten to planned giving follow-up. Has not written impact note for local contractor who funded the ultrasound probe.

Frustration: every "small" task depends on memory. Her memory, mostly. Knows who should be thanked, who should be asked, who needs space, who is quietly drifting away. But cannot scale her own attention.

On her mind: "We are too small for enterprise software, but too busy to keep running on instinct."

## Top 3 frustrations

1. Institutional memory lives in Heather's head
2. Small team means every missed thank-you feels personal
3. Data cleanup loses to urgent community asks

## Tools used daily

- CRM: DonorPerfect or RE NXT
- Email: Mailchimp, Outlook
- Analytics: Excel, built-in CRM reports
- Prospect research: CharityCAN when budget allows, Google, board knowledge
- Most screen time: Outlook and donor lookup
- Tab she wishes she could close: CanadaHelps export

## Where she spends attention

AFP chapter, AHP webinars, CAGP, local hospital foundation peer group. Rare LinkedIn poster, reads local foundation news. Advice circle: Board chair, hospital CEO, one ED at a nearby foundation, finance manager.

## What makes her say yes

- **Demo trigger:** AI4Love finds 20 real people who need attention this week, without asking her to rebuild the database
- **Champion moment:** Produces a board-safe explanation of why those 20 matter
- **Killer objection:** Expensive setup, long implementation, or central IT dependency
- **Unspoken concern:** A larger vendor will make her feel unsophisticated

## Buying authority

Up to $10K CAD alone. $10K-$25K requires board chair or Finance. Over $25K or multi-year requires board approval. Blessing: Board chair, finance committee, hospital privacy contact if data access involved.

## Triggering events

- Organizational: gala or campaign creates hundreds of new engagement records, donor retention slips, staff vacancy exposes lack of process
- Personal: misses a stewardship moment, feels buried before a board meeting, sees a peer foundation using smarter donor follow-up

## Quote

> "I know the people. I need the system to remember with me."

## How AI4Love positioning lands

- **Master category:** moderate. "Intelligence layer" is slightly abstract for her. Lead with concrete "20 people this week" framing instead.
- **Strategic inversion:** weak. Heather's stack is small; centralization vs decentralization is not her tension.
- **Behavioral doctrine:** very strong. "We surface. We never send." aligns with her instinct that relationships are personal.

## How the three refusals land

1. **No write-back to systems:** moderate. She would probably welcome write-back; the protection is more for Marc-André's risks.
2. **No campaign launches:** strong. Reassures her that the tool will not steal her judgment.
3. **No sending to supporters:** very strong. Heather's whole value system is personal handwritten notes and recognition. Anything automated would feel wrong.

## Strategic note

Heather is Motion B / Tier Core. Her demo cannot lead with architecture. It must lead with relief. "Nine agents" matters less than "here are the 20 people worth your attention this week." Phase 2 synthesis flags this explicitly.
