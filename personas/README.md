# Buyer Personas

Six personas built from Phase 1 research and Phase 2 ChatGPT synthesis. Use in sales conversations, demo prep, content planning, and product decisions.

The **killer objections** and **unspoken concerns** are the most important fields. Never make a claim that triggers a killer objection. Always design sales motion to defuse the unspoken concern without naming it.

## Anti-patterns to avoid (the six killer objections)

Never claim, imply, or roadmap any of these.

1. "We need a full database export" (kills Sarah)
2. "We write insight fields back into your CRM automatically" (kills Marc-André)
3. "We automate the next appeal" (kills Kimberly)
4. "This requires every unit to change workflow first" (kills David)
5. "Expensive setup, long implementation, central IT dependency" (kills Heather)
6. "Any tool that drafts donor-facing content without human review" (kills Emily)

The three locked refusals in [../refusals/](../refusals/README.md) directly answer objections 2, 3, and 6.

## Unspoken concerns to defuse

None of these will appear in a discovery call. All will silently kill deals.

- **Sarah:** "Another tool my tired team will quietly ignore." Defuse: prove low team load and immediate value.
- **Marc-André:** "Blamed if AI insight embarrasses a dean." Defuse: traceable insight lineage, suppression-first.
- **Kimberly:** "AI making annual giving look less strategic, not more." Defuse: elevate the role, do not replace judgment.
- **David:** "Triggering a political fight with deans." Defuse: respect unit autonomy, no workflow change required.
- **Heather:** "Feeling unsophisticated next to a big vendor." Defuse: peer language, light-touch onboarding.
- **Emily:** "AI flattening the college's voice." Defuse: preserve institutional voice, drafts not sends.

## Synthesis pattern

AI4Love is not really selling AI. It is selling coordination.

The strongest buyers are already surrounded by tools. Their pain is not missing software. It is missing shared attention. In large institutions, the same supporter appears in too many partial views. AI4Love is valuable when it reduces political friction between units, not when it promises better predictions.

Governance is not a blocker. It is a wedge. The zero-custody, no-automated-outreach, suppression-first model speaks directly to the Trust-Layer buyer. That buyer kills weak AI tools before a VP ever sees them.

The tension: Operational Champions (Kimberly, Emily) want relief fast. Trust-Layer buyers (Marc-André) want control, lineage, and safety. The sales motion needs both proof and restraint.

For small shops (Heather, Emily): the product has to feel lighter than the problem. "Nine agents" matters less than "here are the 20 people worth your attention this week."
